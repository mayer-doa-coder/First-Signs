\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
This report presents the requirements, architecture, implementation, feasibility, comparative position, and repository-level evaluation of \product, a multilingual, mobile-first screening and care-navigation prototype for Bangladesh. The platform supports five equally represented, non-diagnostic pathways: persistent cough or respiratory concern; fever or mosquito-borne illness concern; diarrhoea or dehydration concern; skin, sensation, or nerve concern; and child cough or difficult breathing. Its central contribution is a closed-loop workflow connecting consent, deterministic warning-sign screening, facility discovery, optional referral, independent attendance verification, missed-visit follow-up, pharmacy-scoped simulated incentives, and privacy-preserving public-health investigation signals.

Safety outcomes are generated by fixed, versioned TypeScript rules. Optional artificial-intelligence and acoustic services are prevented by design from overriding those outcomes. The implementation uses Next.js 16, React 19, strict TypeScript, Supabase/PostgreSQL, MapLibre, QR tooling, and optional Gemini, Telegram, Resend, and Google HeAR integrations. Repository evaluation performed on 19 October 2026 found that linting, TypeScript checking, and the production build completed successfully; 38 focused automated test cases also passed. These findings establish software consistency in the evaluated scope, not clinical effectiveness. The prototype is technically feasible for continued research, while deployment remains contingent on expert review, verified multi-pathway facility data, accessibility and usability studies, operational partnerships, privacy governance, security assessment, and prospective field evaluation.

\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Project Overview}
People can be lost at every handoff between noticing a warning sign and reaching formal care: interpreting symptoms, finding an appropriate service, preserving a referral, attending the visit, and recovering after a missed visit. Bangladesh research reports delayed care seeking and frequent initial contact with nearby pharmacies among people with tuberculosis symptoms \cite{ahmed2025delay}. Digital symptom-checker studies also report variable triage and diagnostic performance \cite{semigran2015symptom,wallace2022accuracy,riboli2023triage}. These findings motivate a system that is explicit about its limits and optimized for safe navigation rather than diagnosis.

\product addresses this discontinuity through a closed loop:
\begin{center}\textbf{Concern $\rightarrow$ safe guidance $\rightarrow$ facility $\rightarrow$ referral $\rightarrow$ attendance $\rightarrow$ follow-up.}\end{center}
The architecture is multilingual; the present implementation provides complete English and Bangla interfaces. All five pathways are first-class entry points. Respiratory audio research and the current TB-evaluation facility directory are bounded components, not the identity of the platform.

\subsection{Problem Statement}
The target problem is not simply insufficient health information. It is the loss of continuity between early concern and completed formal evaluation. A standalone symptom questionnaire may produce advice without a destination; a verbal recommendation may be forgotten; a missed appointment may end the journey; and an aggregate dashboard may expose more patient data than programme teams need. The engineering problem is to connect these transitions while preserving safety, patient choice, role separation, and minimum-data principles.

\subsection{Objectives and Scope}
The objectives are to describe the multi-pathway requirements and implementation; document deterministic safety, privacy, incentive, and failure boundaries; compare the design with related research and common system archetypes; report repository experiments without overstating them; and identify evidence needed for responsible continuation. The scope excludes diagnosis, prescribing, clinical validation, epidemiological claims, regulatory approval, deployment economics, and real-world effectiveness claims.

\section{Research Background, Requirements, and Design}
\subsection{Related Works}
Digital-triage research establishes a cautious baseline. \emph{Evaluation of Symptom Checkers for Self Diagnosis and Triage: Audit Study} reported substantial variation across public systems \cite{semigran2015symptom}. \emph{The Diagnostic and Triage Accuracy of Digital and Online Symptom Checker Tools: A Systematic Review} \cite{wallace2022accuracy} and \emph{Triage and Diagnostic Accuracy of Online Symptom Checkers: Systematic Review} \cite{riboli2023triage} likewise found variable performance and limited evidence for safe broad adoption. This supports small, inspectable rules for care-action categories while keeping generative AI outside safety decisions.

WHO states that digital interventions supplement rather than replace health systems \cite{who2019digital}; WHO SMART Guidelines emphasize standards-based, machine-readable, adaptive, requirements-based, and testable specifications \cite{whoSmart}. \product follows that direction conceptually through language-neutral outcomes and versioned rules without claiming formal conformance.

\subsection{Requirement Analysis}
Functional requirements include two entry modes; consent; five pathway-specific screens; urgent precedence; language-neutral results; facility discovery; optional referral; referral ID, token, and QR; independent attendance confirmation; private care history; missed-visit support; pharmacy-scoped metrics; reward safeguards; anonymous community feedback; and grouped programme signals.

Non-functional requirements include mobile responsiveness, readable health content, equivalent English/Bangla meaning, server-side validation, minimum-data collection, scoped authorization, idempotent state changes, graceful degradation, secret isolation, and regression-testability. Merriweather is required for English and Noto Sans Bengali for Bangla.

\subsection{Methodology}
Development follows a requirements-driven, safety-first method: define pathway danger signs and outputs; encode language-neutral rules with urgent precedence; separate translated presentation from calculation; validate at client and server boundaries; separate private records from minimum signals; model referral, verification, follow-up, and rewards as controlled transitions; isolate optional integrations; and connect critical boundaries to tests. Explainability is treated as inspectability: a reviewer can locate a rule version, validation, outcome code, display copy, persistence function, and regression test.

\subsection{Deterministic Decision Design}
Each pathway maps validated inputs to one of three language-neutral outcomes:
\begin{align*}
O \in \{&\texttt{CONTINUE\_WITH\_CARE\_INFORMATION},\\
&\texttt{PROFESSIONAL\_EVALUATION\_RECOMMENDED},\\
&\texttt{URGENT\_MEDICAL\_EVALUATION}\}.
\end{align*}
Urgent danger signs are evaluated first. Fever rules do not guess dengue versus malaria; skin/nerve rules do not diagnose leprosy; and child-breathing rules do not label pneumonia.

\subsection{Referral, Verification, and Follow-Up}
Referral creation is optional and transactional. A public care identifier supports communication while an opaque token protects private reopening. Attendance means formal evaluation started; it is not a diagnosis, test result, or treatment outcome. Missed visits record structured barriers and return practical next actions.

\subsection{Authentication, Transactions, and Integrity}
Supabase Auth provides sessions; server routes resolve role and scope. RLS protects exposed tables and narrowly granted functions perform controlled changes. Privileged keys remain server-side. Transactional counters prevent rolled-back or retried requests from consuming referral numbers. Repeated rewards, audio hashes, referral reuse, and unusual activity lead to narrow review states, never denial of care.

\subsection{Optional Services and Failure Engineering}
If Gemini is unavailable, screening and referral continue. If HeAR decoding, inference, or connectivity fails, symptom-based care continues. If route drawing fails, facility details remain available. If reminder delivery fails, referral state does not change. Telegram follows the same server-owned rules.

Technical feasibility is supported by the successful build, typed logic, validation, migrations, and focused checks. Operational feasibility is conditional on onboarding, facility acceptance, service ownership, and reliable identity. \subsection{Discussion}
The closed loop links information to action: guidance can lead to a destination, referral can be independently completed, a missed visit can return to navigation, and programme signals can be derived without publishing private records. Deterministic rules trade predictive flexibility for auditability. At this stage, urgent precedence and language-neutral codes are easier to review and test than an opaque predictor.

The privacy design minimizes the signal rather than attempting to anonymize a complete record. Broad-area signals may support investigation or capacity planning, but cannot establish prevalence or a confirmed outbreak. Real deployment would need minimum cell sizes, disclosure control, temporal smoothing, governance, and epidemiological validation.

\subsection{Challenges}
Challenges include preserving simple language without weakening urgent instructions; maintaining equivalent translations; distinguishing overlapping pathways without diagnosis; verifying service capabilities beyond the TB-centred directory; preventing incentive distortion; maintaining cross-channel safety; governing audio data; and separating a polished prototype from an approved clinical system.

\subsection{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
The project lacks clinical, public-health, accessibility, security, legal, and regulatory approval. Content requires independent review. Facility coverage is incomplete. The staff PIN is a prototype control, credits are simulated, and human escalation does not alert a real team. Acoustic work is investigational and lacks the authorized data, calibration, subgroup analysis, and external validation needed for patient-facing triage.

The reported checks do not validate thresholds, translations, live RLS policies, facility accuracy, third-party availability, accessibility, load, or clinical outcomes. Browser suites, SQL regressions, live Supabase and optional-service checks, penetration testing, and human-subject studies were outside this report run. Controlled fixtures are regression evidence, not external-service validation.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\subsection{Conclusion}
\product demonstrates a coherent approach to multi-syndrome care navigation in Bangladesh. It integrates deterministic safety guidance, destination finding, optional portable referral, independent attendance confirmation, barrier-aware follow-up, scoped incentives, and privacy-safe signals. The repository passed the focused static, build, and unit checks executed for this report. It remains a promising and testable prototype, not a diagnostic device or clinically validated service.

\subsection{Future Scope}
Priority work includes independent clinical, public-health, and language review; usability studies; verified pathway-specific facility data; accessibility and low-bandwidth testing; production identity, audit, monitoring, and incident response; disclosure controls for programme reporting; prospective measurement of referral acceptance and attendance; governed incentive and cost-effectiveness studies; interoperability; additional fully reviewed languages; load and penetration testing; and separately governed acoustic research using authorized data and preregistered evaluation.
